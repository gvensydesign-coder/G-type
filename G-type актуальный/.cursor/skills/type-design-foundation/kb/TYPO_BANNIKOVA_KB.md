# Галина Банникова — база знаний (typejournal.ru/articles/galina-bannikova)

Source: «Галина Банникова» article, typejournal.ru (read 2026-11-01; the Kama section was not readable).

## Person and context
- Г. А. Банникова (1901–1972), ВХУТЕМАС 1926–1930; from 1939 — type laboratory НИИ ОГИЗ РСФСР.
- Soviet type stock after OST 1337 was poor and pre-revolutionary (Латинская, Обыкновенная, Кориnна, Академическая): coarse, over-contrasted, hard to read or pretentious.
- Defects she named while reworking Литературная (to AVOID): monotonous rhythm, weak stroke modulation, mechanical skeleton, dry uniform drawing.

## Банниковская (1949; digital ParaType 1999 / 2017, L. Kuznetsova)
- Goal: unite the graphic logic of the early-18th-c. Petrine civil type with Renaissance old-style; a dynamic old-style with enough contrast for long text at small sizes.
- Rhythm = the main style-forming factor.
- **Width variation** (разноширинность) is the main virtue of the Russian civil type. The 1951 readability study (Ушакова/Артёмов, 10 pt: Банниковская vs Литературная, Академическая, Елизаветинская) ranked Банниковская 1st, and width variation measurably improved legibility of letters and words.
- Proportion work: letter widths, x-height, cap height, lowercase:caps ratio. Proportions fitted for Cyrillic had to be re-adjusted for Latin, not copied.
- **Petrine variant forms**:
  - ж is not built as two mirrored к;
  - drop terminals of а к л differ from the terminals of с ж;
  - б: bowl and ascender share one spine, and the bowl meets it in a rigid joint;
  - descenders of д ц щ are SLANTED, not copies of the vertical serifs of т г. This avoids the «частокол» (picket-fence) look.
- Archaic alternates in 1949 proofs (later dropped as too extravagant): a big-oval Р sitting on the baseline with a descender, a descending У and Ф, and old-style (minuscule) figures.
- Method:
  - freehand drawing;
  - judges apertures, counters and black/white balance by eye, the glyph as ONE black-white unit (not «form first, aperture second»);
  - roman and italic drawn together and tested in words;
  - accented letters sketched for national alphabets.
- **Ink spread / optical gain** hurts narrow letters more (it fills narrow counters), so contrast and counters of narrow glyphs are tuned with this in mind.
- Italic: she tried 18th-c. forms (an А from Lomonosov 1757, a slanted п for в) and rejected those that were technically or legibility-unsuitable.
- Initials: two series (bold letters; reversed letters on a black rounded rectangle) with combinable ornamental «приставки».

## Classification by construction, not by serif
- Two poles:
  - **circle-based** (round letters from a circle; secondary features follow: low contrast, gradual thickening, rounded/bracketed serifs, drop terminals, slanted serif joins → old-style);
  - **vertical-based** (built on the vertical stem → modern/didone).
- Slab serifs and grotesques attach to these two poles.
- «Классифицировать шрифты по форме засечек» is like classifying animals by leg shape while ignoring the skeleton.
- Width logic in caps: if О is a circle, a NARROW Р fits it; small-radius bowls give narrow letters (В), large radii wide ones (О), so old-style cap widths are naturally uneven.

## Байконур (= «Банниковская вторая», 1960; specimen 1966)
- A modern-style (vertical-group) book antiqua made readable at small sizes.
- **Entasis**: a slight smooth swelling at the middle of the main strokes.
- Reduced contrast while keeping classicist features.
- Strict verticals; vertical ovals with squarish inner counters.
- Massive short serifs joined at a slight angle.
- Thickened connecting strokes «чтобы выдержать единство построения гарнитуры».
- Italic designed for small sizes and keeping the roman's unusual solutions.

## Rules extracted for G-type
1. Decide the skeleton (circle vs vertical) first; contrast, serif and terminal types must FOLLOW from it, not be mixed freely.
2. Keep deliberate width variation in text faces (no equal-width «частокол»), unless a display concept demands equal widths (e.g. a condensed constructivist face).
3. Avoid the «частокол»: vary the descenders (slanted д ц щ) and terminal types between letter groups.
4. Compensate narrow letters and small counters for optical gain (wider counter or lower contrast in narrow glyphs).
5. Draw and test roman+italic and Cyrillic+Latin together, in words; re-proportion Latin rather than copying Cyrillic metrics.
6. For high-contrast faces meant for smaller sizes: entasis, squarish inner counters, shorter massive serifs, thickened joins.
7. Offer historical alternates (Petrine Р У Ф, old-style figures) as OpenType stylistic sets rather than defaults.
